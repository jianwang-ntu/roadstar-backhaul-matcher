# RoadStar backhaul matcher

Deadhead reduction for freight backhaul, built for RoadStar Hackathon 2026.

A tractor that delivers a load and then drives empty to its next pickup is
running **deadhead**. This project decides which waiting load each just-emptied
truck should take, subject to trailer compatibility, pickup time windows and
hours-of-service, and it does so against the objective a carrier actually has —
contribution per truck — rather than against nearest-load distance.

---

## Read this before any number below

**Every result in this repository is computed on synthetic instances.** The
geography is real (public city coordinates for the southern-Ontario / Quebec /
border-crossing corridor, `roadstar/geo.py`); the trucks, loads, time windows
and revenues are generated by a seeded generator, `roadstar/instance.py`.

The event's own freight / order / trip / truck-spec tables sit behind the
organiser's participant portal, which requires creating an account. No portal
data has been read, so no result here is portal-derived. `load_portal_instance`
is the seam where that data attaches; it **raises** rather than falling back to
synthetic data, so no run can ever report portal numbers it did not have.

Nothing in this README is claimed as a measurement of a real carrier's fleet.

## The accounting boundary, before the table

Three boundary facts decide how every number below should be read. All three
were found by independent adversarial audits of earlier revisions of this
repository, and all three are stated here rather than in a footnote.

1. **Hours of service now charges the whole duty day.** It has been wrong twice,
   both times in the flattering direction, and both corrections are measured
   rather than described:

   | version of C3 | what it charged | dispatches it cleared that the current rule calls illegal | worst duty day |
   |---|---|---:|---:|
   | before round-1 finding D1 | empty repositioning drive only | **530 of 1145 — 46.29%** | **29.38 h** |
   | as round 2 audited it | both drives, no shipper wait | **379 of 1096 — 34.58%** | **21.88 h** |
   | **this revision** | both drives **+ the wait at the shipper** + pickup overhead | — | **14.00 h** |

   The middle row is round-2 finding DOMAIN-01. Time spent waiting at the
   shipper for the pickup window to open is on-duty not-driving under both
   SOR/2005-313 and FMCSA 395.2; the previous revision charged none of it, and
   the mean wait on its accepted dispatches was 190 minutes. A ceiling that
   binds on part of the duty day is not a ceiling, and the residual it left was
   *larger* than the D1 defect the previous README led with as its correction.
   Every figure in that table is regenerated by
   `tests/test_hos_binds_the_whole_duty_day.py`, so it fails CI rather than
   ageing quietly.

   The previous revision reported the D1 severity as "14.0%". That figure does
   not reproduce under any aggregation and has been withdrawn, not restated —
   round-2 finding CLAIMS-02.

2. **One charity in the hours-of-service model, counted.** A wait of 8 h or more
   is treated as the qualifying off-duty period that ends a shift, so it is not
   charged; the model does not check that the driver actually took it off duty.
   **184 of `profit_assignment`'s 1051 accepted dispatches (17.51%) depend on
   that reading.** Prior on-duty time is *not* forgiven by the same reset, which
   is the conservative direction. Both facts are asserted by the suite.

3. **The post-delivery run home is outside the primary boundary.** A truck that
   takes no backhaul is charged its empty run to domicile; a truck that *does*
   take one is not charged the empty run home from where it drops the load.
   That is a single-period truncation and it flatters matching. So the primary
   empty fractions here are **lower bounds** and the primary profits are
   **upper bounds**, and the fully symmetric figures — charging every truck a
   run home — are printed in the same table. Under the symmetric boundary every
   policy is loss-making and `profit_assignment` is not the best policy;
   `greedy_profit_pairwise` is. That is reported because it is what the runs
   did.

## Results

40 trucks × 50 loads, 30 seeded instances (`make sweep`, `results/sweep_40x50.json`).
Every policy sees the identical instances and is scored by the identical
feasibility rules, so the comparison is paired.

| policy | mean deadhead km | empty fraction | mean profit | matched | illegal proposed | empty fraction *incl. run home* | profit *incl. run home* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `naive_nearest` | 7718.6 | 75.03% | -$4,949 | 13.5 | **795** | 81.37% | −$8,652 |
| `greedy_feasible` (distance baseline) | 4872.5 | 42.15% | $3,439 | 31.9 | 0 | 65.68% | −$5,204 |
| `greedy_profit_pairwise` (profit baseline) | 4157.9 | 34.21% | $5,321 | 32.9 | 0 | **61.79%** | **−$4,189** |
| `optimal_assignment` | 4391.4 | 35.64% | $5,379 | 35.8 | 0 | 63.31% | −$4,751 |
| **`profit_assignment`** | **3996.1** | **31.89%** | **$6,172** | 35.0 | 0 | 61.61% | −$4,390 |

Paired, per instance. `mean` is the mean of per-instance ratios; `ratio of
means` is the same comparison computed on the aggregates. Both are given
because they disagree sharply here, and the disagreement is the point — see the
note under the table:

| comparison | metric | mean | median | sd | ratio of means | wins/ties/losses | worst instance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `profit_assignment` vs `greedy_profit_pairwise` | profit | **+18.207%** | +14.886% | 11.2 | +15.988% | 30 / 0 / 0 | +6.497% |
| `greedy_profit_pairwise` vs `greedy_feasible` | profit | +109.046% | +43.935% | 212.6 | +54.723% | 30 / 0 / 0 | +10.567% |
| `profit_assignment` vs `greedy_feasible` | profit | +151.993% | +62.485% | 290.4 | +79.460% | 30 / 0 / 0 | +33.150% |
| `profit_assignment` vs `optimal_assignment` | profit | +27.245% | +12.094% | 64.1 | +14.751% | 29 / **1** / 0 | +0.000% |
| `optimal_assignment` vs `greedy_feasible` | deadhead | +9.466% | +8.458% | 12.3 | +9.874% | 28 / 0 / **2** | **−31.498%** |
| `profit_assignment` vs `greedy_feasible` | deadhead | +17.457% | +15.510% | 12.4 | +17.987% | 29 / 0 / **1** | **−1.163%** |

**Do not quote the three-figure profit percentages.** `greedy_feasible` earns
$3,439 on average and much less than that on some instances, so a mean of
per-instance ratios against it is dominated by its near-zero denominators: the
best single instance reads **+1527%**, the sd is 290 points, and the median is
**+62%**. The defensible statements against the distance baseline are the
median (+62.485%), the ratio of means (+79.460%) and the absolute figure —
**$3,439 → $6,172 of mean profit per instance.** Against the *profit* baseline,
where the denominator is not near zero, the three statistics agree to within
four points and **+18.207%** can be read as-is.

**Read the profit rows together, not the third alone.** The gap between a
distance-greedy dispatcher and this solver is two separate effects added up:
changing the objective from distance to contribution (the second row, and a
24-line greedy gets it), and then solving that objective exactly instead of
greedily (**+18.207%**, the first row). The exact solver's own contribution is
the first number. Reporting only the total would credit an assignment solver
with a gain that mostly comes from scoring the right thing.

At 100 × 120 over 10 instances (`results/sweep_100x120.json`):
`profit_assignment` reaches **20.47%** empty fraction and **$22,012** mean
profit, **+11.921%** over `greedy_profit_pairwise` (10/0/0) and +40.270% over
`greedy_feasible` (10/0/0). One sweep of 10 instances runs in ~2 s.

**Where the empty-fraction improvement comes from.** The empty fraction is a
ratio, and **most of `profit_assignment`'s improvement in it is denominator
growth, not emptier trucks.** Against `greedy_feasible` the empty fraction falls
10.26 points, of which **4.55 points come from driving fewer empty kilometres
and 5.71 points — 55.6% — from hauling more loaded ones.** The loaded-kilometre
denominator grows 27.91% while the matched count grows only 9.94%, because the
policy also selects *longer* loads: mean matched load length rises from 208.2 km
to 242.2 km. Round-2 findings CLAIMS-05 and DOMAIN-05: the previous revision
attributed this to matching more trucks, which is a third of the mechanism.
Absolute deadhead kilometres do fall, from 4872.5 to 3996.1 (−18.0%), and both
absolute deadhead and profit are in the table so the ratio cannot be read alone.

## The three things that make this more than a nearest-load lookup

**1. One definition of legal, applied to every policy.** `roadstar/feasibility.py`
is the only place a dispatch is judged: trailer compatibility (C1), pickup
window (C2), hours of service (C3). The naive baseline is scored by the same
function, which is why its 795 illegal proposals are visible instead of being
quietly counted as matches. A comparison where the baseline is allowed to cheat
is not a comparison.

**2. The outside option is priced.** A truck that takes no backhaul does not
vanish from the cost base — it runs empty to its domicile. `profit_assignment`
scores every candidate pairing against *that* alternative:

```
v_ij = revenue_j − cost_per_km × (deadhead_ij + loaded_km_j) + cost_per_km × reposition_i
```

so it can correctly **decline** a load that is worth less than sending the truck
home. Round-2 finding CLAIMS-03: the previous revision claimed this policy was
"the only policy here that can", and that was false against this repository's
own code — `greedy_profit_pairwise` applies the identical `v > 0` filter to the
identical objective and declines *more* (12 trucks left standing beside a legal
unused load, against `profit_assignment`'s 8, over 1200 trucks). Declining is a
property of the objective, not of the exact solve. It is the two
*distance*-scored policies that cannot decline, and both leave 0.

**3. Exact, not heuristic — and measured against a heuristic that optimises the
same thing.** The assignment is solved exactly (Jonker–Volgenant via
`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment`). Optimality is checked against
brute-force enumeration on small instances, and the oracle derives the matching
*cardinality* itself rather than accepting the solver's — an earlier version
took the cardinality from the solver, which let a mutant matching one load pass
the entire suite. The rejected big-M formulation is kept executable in
`tests/test_policies.py::test_big_m_formulation_is_rejected_by_the_oracle`, so
"the oracle catches it" is a test that runs, not a sentence in this file. Which
instances discriminate the two formulations depends on the feasibility rule, so
it was re-measured when C3 changed: at 5 × 5 over seeds 800–829 the shipped
formulation is strictly better at seeds **825 and 827**, and the test asserts
exactly that pair. At 4 × 4, the size the previous revision used, the corrected
rule leaves the two formulations tied on all 30 seeds and the test would be
vacuous.

## Verify it

```
pip install -r requirements.txt
make test      # 76 tests
make sweep     # regenerates results/*.json
make dispatch  # prints the actual truck -> load dispatch for one instance
```

The suite covers: haversine against an independent WGS-84 Vincenty solver
(agreement within 0.5%, the sphere-vs-ellipsoid bound); the HOS ceilings at
their exact boundary minute, pinned to the regulated literals rather than to the
constants under test; that every accepted dispatch of every constraint-aware
policy fits inside a duty day **recomputed from primitives without consulting
the feasibility code**, over the whole headline sweep; that the two retired
versions of C3 are measurably illegal under the current one; the trailer matrix
in both directions; that no constraint-aware policy ever returns an illegal
dispatch across 30 seeds; both solvers against brute force; that the profit
baseline declines loads too; that a policy matching *nothing* scores worst
rather than best on the empty-mile metric; that unmatched trucks are charged
their repositioning kilometres; byte-identical CLI output across runs; that
every number in the tables above equals the committed results JSON; and that the
portal loader raises instead of inventing data.

Deleting the loaded leg from C3, deleting the shipper wait from C3, or raising
either ceiling all turn the suite red. Under the previous revision the first and
third of those passed all 52 tests — round-2 findings DOMAIN-02 and DOMAIN-03.

## What this does not do

- **No routed distances.** Road distance is the geodesic × a fixed 1.20 circuity
  factor, applied uniformly, so it cancels out of every ratio reported above and
  shifts only absolute kilometre totals. It is not a road network, and whether
  1.20 is the right constant for this corridor is untested here.
- **Partial HOS.** Daily driving (13 h) and on-duty (14 h) ceilings only, with
  the shipper wait charged and the 8 h reset described above. No 70 h/7 day
  cycle, no sleeper-berth split, no deferral, no US rules once a truck crosses
  at Windsor or Buffalo. The 16 h elapsed-shift window is not implemented
  separately because under this accounting it can never bind — every minute it
  would count is already charged against the lower 14 h ceiling. Nothing here
  asserts regulatory compliance.
- **Single period.** One round of matching, not a rolling multi-day plan. The
  post-delivery empty leg falls outside that boundary; see the accounting note
  above and the `incl. run home` columns.
- **A degenerate instance family.** Trucks and load origins are drawn from the
  same 19 corridor nodes, so a large share of pairs have exactly zero deadhead,
  and the synthetic rate card ($1.85/km + $120) has no variance across lanes.
  Both make the matching problem easier than a real book of business. **Not
  fixed in this revision** and it caps how much the comparisons above can be
  read as a product claim.
- **`$1.10/km` operating cost is a stated assumption**, not a measured cost base.
  Profit is revenue *minus* cost × km, so it does **not** simply scale with the
  constant — the constant moves the relative comparisons non-proportionally.
  `--cost-per-km` re-runs the whole sweep at another value; the sensitivity has
  **not** been swept and reported here yet.
- **The rate card is not defended against real truckload economics.** Round-2
  finding DOMAIN-09 measured an implied operating ratio of 0.63 against an
  industry 0.90–0.97. The synthetic instances are more profitable than the
  business is. **Not fixed in this revision.**
- **No integration with the event's provided APIs or datasets** (Samsara, Truck
  Mate, Motive, DAT, Loadlink) — see the access note above. This is judging
  criterion 4 and it is **unmet**.
- **The demo video is not on a video platform, and the deck has no slot to be
  presented in.** The 3–5 minute demo video the rules ask for is committed here,
  at `demo/roadstar_demo.mp4` — 4 min 25 s, built from `script(1)` recordings of
  real runs of commit `153deaf`, sha256 `5b9c850a`. It has **not been uploaded
  to YouTube, Vimeo or any other video-hosting platform**, and **no entry has
  been filed anywhere**. Judging criterion 5 also asks for a 10–15 minute live
  presentation. The material for it is committed at `presentation/` — 13 slides
  and a 13 min 10 s timed script — but **a deck is not a presentation**: this
  entrant is in Singapore, the session is in person in Waterloo, and whether a
  remote entrant may present at all is unpublished. Criterion 5 stays
  **unmet**.

## What the independent audits found, and what is still open

This repository has been audited adversarially twice by reviewers holding only
the code and the organiser's published rules — not the plan, not the build
notes. Round 1 returned MAJOR_REVISION_REQUIRED at 37.3/100 over 15 defects.
Round 2 ran three lenses over the revision that answered it and returned
NOT_FIT_TO_SUBMIT at a mean of 44.7/100 over 34 findings — a *lower* score than
round 1, because the round-1 revision had closed defects instance by instance
while leaving their classes open.

This revision closes the five findings that made the shipped repository say
things that were not true:

- **DOMAIN-01** — C3 charged no shipper waiting time, so 42.79% of accepted
  dispatches blew the on-duty ceiling and the worst duty day was 24.45 h. The
  wait is now charged; the worst duty day is 14.00 h; the correction and its
  severity are asserted by the suite.
- **DOMAIN-02 / DOMAIN-03** — reverting the loaded leg, or setting both ceilings
  to 30 h, passed all 52 tests of the previous revision's suite. Both now fail,
  against an oracle that rebuilds the duty day from primitives and compares it
  against the regulated numbers written out as literals.
- **CLAIMS-01** — the prose said the naive baseline made 528 illegal proposals
  while the table beside it said 627 and no run produced 528. The figure is now
  795, it comes from the results JSON, and CI compares the two.
- **CLAIMS-02** — the "14.0%" severity cited for the pre-D1 HOS defect does not
  reproduce. Withdrawn and replaced by a measured, regenerated table.
- **CLAIMS-03** — "It is the only policy here that can [decline a load]" was
  false against this repository's own `greedy_profit_pairwise`. Corrected, and
  now asserted by a test.
- **CLAIMS-04** — the README-versus-results guard checked only the flattering
  columns, so a fabricated profit-baseline row, fabricated symmetric-boundary
  columns and a fabricated 100 × 120 paragraph all passed CI. All three are now
  covered.

Still open, stated rather than closed quietly:

- **The organiser's submission portal has not been opened.** Not agent-closable:
  it needs a human to create the participant account. The 3–5 minute demo video
  the rules demand exists and is committed here at `demo/roadstar_demo.mp4`,
  built from `script(1)` recordings of commit `153deaf`; the presentation deck
  exists at `presentation/` but has no slot to be delivered in, the video is on
  no video-hosting platform, and no entry has been filed anywhere.
- **Judging criterion 4 (provided data and APIs, 15% of the score) is unmet**,
  and criterion 5 (presentation, 15%) is unmet. Both are in the organiser's own
  rubric and neither is claimed here.
- **The cost-constant sensitivity is not swept** (round-1 D7); the flag exists.
- **The instance family is degenerate and the rate card is not defensible**
  (round-1 D8, round-2 DOMAIN-09), as described above.
- **`optimal_assignment` is not optimal for the deadhead figure it is tabled
  on** (round-2 DOMAIN-04). It minimises matched deadhead only, which is the
  defect `profit_assignment` exists to fix; the name is kept because the results
  JSON and both audits refer to it, and the limitation is stated here instead.
- **Round-2 findings not listed above remain open**, including the relabelling
  of the accounting boundary (RUBRIC-05) and the absence of dispersion on the
  symmetric-boundary columns (RUBRIC-11).
